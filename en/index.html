About the Workshop

Leave behind everything that keeps you from writing

This workshop exists to break the "I don't know how to write" freeze. No theory, no pressure — just a prompt, some silence, and the surprise of what comes out. Over four weeks we write together, then read our words aloud.

Program

Four weeks, four doors

Each week stands on its own — you can join any week. No theory, no pressure; just starting to write.

Week 1

Out of Your Head, Onto the Page

We start with word clouds. You catch words as they appear in short videos, then collide an adjective with a noun ("Happiness is always exciting") and either defend or argue it. Sentences emerge almost by accident. Your material is already there.

  • Word clouds
  • Defend it or argue it
  • Pick a word, cloud outward
Week 2

What You Hold

We write from a single word: hold. What do you hold physically every day? What do you hold emotionally? And what do you hold for someone else — family, inheritance, expectation? The move from concrete to interior is how good writing works.

  • What do you hold each day?
  • Physical → emotional
  • What you hold for others
Week 3

The Story Inside the Ordinary

A real novel's title becomes your doorway: "Today a Woman Went Mad in the Supermarket." We talk about it briefly, then you choose one of three titles and write into it. The extraordinary hides inside the ordinary; memoir and fiction blur here.

  • Choose one of three titles
  • The ordinary cracking open
  • Between memory and fiction
Week 4

A Poem From What You Gathered

We end playfully and structurally: an A-to-Z poem. The constraint is oddly freeing — people who say they "can't write poetry" find themselves doing it. You harvest the words you've collected over four weeks, and we close with a small reading ritual.

  • An A-to-Z poem
  • The harvest of four weeks
  • A closing reading
